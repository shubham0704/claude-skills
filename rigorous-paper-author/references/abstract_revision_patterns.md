# Abstract revision without claim drift

Use when an abstract is accurate but hard to follow, or when the user asks whether
its contribution is clear. Read the current body and evidence before rewriting.
An abstract should orient the intended reader; it is not a fixed sentence template.

## Diagnose before polishing

Identify the reader's problem, the obstacle, the proposed insight, the method's
actual role, and the strongest relevant finding. Distinguish an insight from a
list of modules and a measured finding from a planned experiment. A historical
draft or a skill example is not current experimental evidence.

For a substantial reframing, use `story_first_papers.md` to rehearse the argument
before shortening it. For a local edit, retain the agreed story and scope.

## Preserve meaning when making language concrete

Replace unexplained terminology with its actual consequence, retaining assumptions.
Do not trade a qualified mathematical statement for a stronger everyday phrase.

Example: power-preserving interconnection means that power passed between
components cancels in the combined accounting. It does not mean that a dissipative,
externally driven system conserves its total stored energy. A plain explanation is
"the connections transfer power between components without creating or absorbing
it." Component losses, external supply, and numerical error remain separate.

Likewise, consistency with a model is not identification of the real mechanism;
forecast accuracy is not a demonstrated decision improvement; an offline score
using future-derived inputs is not evidence of deployable counterfactual ranking.
Use a domain-specific equivalence check when simplifying a technical claim.

## Vary the prose without imposing a formula

- Shorten repeated catalogues where representative examples preserve the claim.
- Let concrete consequences explain why a method detail matters.
- Use a question, metaphor, or compact memorable sentence only when it helps this
  audience; none is mandatory. Do not require an italicized slogan.
- Keep terminology consistent in meaning. Requiring an exact verb to appear a
  fixed number of times in the body is not a semantic check.
- Keep the final sentence useful: it may state a result, implication, or scoped
  conclusion. Do not append a broad promise merely to avoid ending on a number.

## Choose results by relevance and reliability

Prioritize evidence that answers the central question, not the most surprising
number. Check the baseline, metric, units, repetition unit, uncertainty, failures,
and data-access conditions needed to interpret the claim. Parameter count alone
does not explain an accuracy gain; correlation alone does not validate an action.
Not all protocol detail belongs in the abstract, but do not omit a qualification
that changes what the result means. Never reuse numerical examples from a skill
as results for the current paper.

## Make the revision inspectable

Before editing, state which claims and supporting facts stay, move, or change.
After editing, compare the revised abstract with the current introduction,
methods, results, and conclusion. Check for stronger scope, hidden assumptions,
lost contributions, and repeated arguments. Match any figures and captions used
to communicate the same idea. Use a first-reader pass for comprehension and a
separate technical pass for equivalence; a smooth retelling does not verify facts.

Adapt to the contribution: a theory result, useful integration, reproduction,
dataset, or negative finding need not mimic a new-method success story.
